# Kiln Log

## Firing
The kiln is fired slowly for the first day, raising the temperature no faster than fifty degrees an hour until the flue glows dull red. Damp ware explodes if the firing is rushed, so the setter checks every shelf by hand before the door is bricked up.

## Quench Bath
When a blade blank comes out of the forge it goes straight into the quench bath, and the timing of the quench decides the hardness of the edge. Three seconds in the bath, out for two, then back until the hissing stops: that timing has been the shop rule for thirty years and no apprentice is permitted to vary it.

## Cracking Faults
Most cracking faults appear along the spine where the steel cooled unevenly. A cracked blank is never reheated and sold; it is marked with chalk, quenched cold, and broken up for scrap so it cannot leave the shop by mistake.
